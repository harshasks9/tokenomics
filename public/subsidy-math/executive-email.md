# Executive email

**Subject: AWS's Claude subsidies are not about to collapse — so let's compete smarter**

Team,

We've all heard it in the field: AWS is throwing ~35% incentives at Anthropic workloads, and surely that can't last. I asked for the numbers to prove it. They didn't. What they show instead is more useful, so here it is straight.

**1. On Claude, AWS is the cashier, not the shop.** Anthropic's IPO filing says that when Claude is sold through a cloud marketplace, Anthropic is the seller of record and sets the price; the cloud "collects" and keeps a fee. Working back from the filing, that fee was ~16% in 2025 and up to ~32% this year. Net of its costs, AWS keeps roughly $25 of every $100 a customer spends on Claude — on Gemini we keep ~$74 before model development costs (~$51 after).

**2. A 35% one-time credit is not a 35% price cut.** For a customer growing 30% a year, it's worth ~10% over three years and ~6% over five. For AWS it pays back in about 13–17 months — if it wins a workload AWS would otherwise lose. That's ordinary customer acquisition, not a broken business model.

**3. Even a permanent 35% isn't crazy for Amazon.** It loses money on the Claude line alone. But ~$5–12 of extra storage, database and networking profit per $100 of AI spend closes the gap. So does Amazon's Anthropic stake, worth ~$289B at the IPO price for ~$18B invested. It only fails if the discount is permanent, fully AWS-funded, and goes to customers AWS would have won anyway.

**4. The reported numbers agree.** AWS earned a 38.7% operating margin last quarter while growing 39%. Microsoft has sold OpenAI models royalty-free since April; its real exposure is OpenAI as a customer (10.8% of its revenue), not resale margin.

**5. The same math applies to us.** When we sell Claude on Vertex, we keep the same thin fee AWS does. And we subsidize too — in our model, Gemini's 50% introductory pricing is roughly break-even in year one once model costs are counted. Anthropic is also at least as big a share of our Cloud backlog (≥22%) as of AWS's (~19%).

**What I want us to do:**

- **Stop saying their model is unsustainable.** It isn't, and customers' CFOs will check. Our credibility is worth more than the line.
- **Never answer a one-time credit with a permanent discount.** Their coupon costs them once; our price cut costs us every year.
- **Match only when it changes the outcome — and lead with Gemini where it fits.** On Gemini, matching a 35% one-time offer pays if it lifts our win odds by ~8% of the base rate (50% → 54%). On resold Claude it needs ~31% (50% → 66%). On Claude, win on platform, guaranteed capacity, data residency and model choice — not credits.
- **Sell the three-year bill.** Put credits, list prices, expected price cuts and what month 13 costs on one page. If list prices keep falling, the credit's expiry mostly costs customers a year of savings — make that visible.
- **Bring me the paperwork, not the headline.** For every competitive deal: incentive form (one-time, credits on growth, recurring, commit-linked), who funds it (AWS, Anthropic, partner), measurement base, term and expiry. We still haven't seen 35% in writing.

The full analysis, the Excel model (every assumption adjustable) and a claims audit of what we can and can't say are on the Subsidy Math site. Push back on any of it.

Thanks,
