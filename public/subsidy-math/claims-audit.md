## What you should not say

Each of these is unsupported or contradicted by the evidence. Saying it would undermine the credibility of everything else.

| Don't say | Why not | Say this instead / note |
|---|---|---|
| "AWS's and Microsoft's AI subsidy model is unsustainable." | Not supported: reported cloud margins are high and rising; time-limited incentives pay back in ~1–1.5 years under base assumptions; Microsoft resells OpenAI royalty-free. | Holds only for permanent, fully cloud-funded discounts on usage the cloud would have won anyway. |
| "AWS loses money on AI" / "Bedrock is unprofitable." | Contradicted: AWS margin 38.7%; management says AI margins are improving; unverified analyst reports say Bedrock lifts AWS's margin. | — |
| "Circular AI investments are fake revenue" or accounting tricks. | No evidence of misconduct; the arrangements are disclosed and contractual. The fair point is concentration and vendor financing (see the defensible-math claims). | — |
| "AWS gives Anthropic customers 35% off, permanently." | No public evidence; MAP credits are growth-based and time-limited; AWS commit discounts reportedly don't apply to Claude. | Individual deals may be richer than policy — verify each. |
| "Gemini is more profitable than Claude on AWS" / "vertical integration guarantees Google wins on cost." | Unprovable from outside: Gemini's development cost and revenue are not disclosed; Gemini holds ~9% of enterprise API spend (survey) vs Anthropic's 55%. | Per-dollar retention is higher on Gemini — say that instead (claim c16). |
| "Microsoft subsidizes OpenAI with below-cost Azure pricing." | No evidence of below-list pricing; Microsoft's exposure is concentration and credit risk in a customer funded by outside capital. | — |
